PRESSURE WASHER BUYING GUIDE

Gas vs. Electric Pressure Washer

Choosing between a gas and electric pressure washer comes down to more than raw PSI. Your cleaning jobs, frequency of use, budget, maintenance tolerance, noise concerns, and required mobility all matter.

For many homeowners, electric is the simpler choice. For frequent, large-area, or professional cleaning, gas can make more sense.

CHOOSE ELECTRIC

Electric is usually better for homeowners

Electric pressure washers make a lot of sense for people who wash cars, clean patios, refresh decks, maintain siding, and perform occasional driveway cleaning.

They typically start easily, require less maintenance than gas machines, and avoid gasoline and engine maintenance. Greenworks currently lists electric models primarily in the $100–$400 range and notes their lower maintenance requirements. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

Best homeowner fit: Choose electric when convenience, low maintenance, lower noise, and everyday residential cleaning are more important than maximum output.
CHOOSE GAS

Gas makes more sense for demanding work

Gas pressure washers are generally the better choice when you regularly clean large areas, tackle stubborn buildup, or need professional-level output.

Consumer Reports notes that the most powerful gas machines can clean significantly faster than many electric models, particularly when large areas or years of accumulated dirt are involved. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

Best professional fit: Choose gas when high output, mobility, and faster large-area cleaning outweigh the extra weight, noise, fuel, and maintenance.
THE REAL QUESTION

How often will you use the pressure washer?

OCCASIONAL

A few times a year

An affordable electric machine is usually the easiest choice. You avoid storing fuel and don't have an engine to maintain between uses.

REGULAR HOMEOWNER

Monthly or weekly

A stronger electric machine can make sense, especially when your jobs include cars, siding, patios, decks, and moderate driveway work.

HEAVY USE

Several times a week

Frequent large-area cleaning starts to favor gas, particularly when high water flow and fast production matter.

ONE MORE THING

Don't compare PSI alone

Pressure is only one part of cleaning performance. GPM tells you how much water the machine moves, and the combination of PSI and GPM gives a better picture of overall output.

Greenworks describes cleaning units as PSI × GPM, which is useful when comparing machines that advertise very different pressure and water-flow combinations. :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}

This is one reason WasherMatch asks about the actual job instead of simply recommending the machine with the biggest PSI number.

BOTTOM LINE

Which should you buy?

If you're a typical homeowner who wants a pressure washer for cars, patios, decks, siding, and occasional outdoor cleaning, start by looking at electric models.

If you're cleaning large surfaces frequently, dealing with heavy buildup, or using the machine professionally, gas becomes much more attractive.
